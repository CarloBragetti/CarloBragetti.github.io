This is not a simple repair: it's the **"blueprint-grade" build** of the 1000 cc V4 from my
Aprilia RSV4 - cleaning, measuring, **mass balancing**, clearance checks and methodical
reassembly. I documented it step by step; here I retrace it with an eye on the *why* of each phase.

*(Note for English readers: the photos carry my original Italian captions from the build diary —
each caption below includes the translation.)*

## STEP 1 - External micro shot-peening

We start with **micro shot-peening** of the crankcases: a delicate micro-blasting that deep-cleans
the aluminium without attacking it, leaving a uniform satin finish. Beyond looks, a clean surface
makes it much easier to **spot cracks, porosity or seepage** during inspection.

Same treatment for the head - the **before/after** comparison says it all:

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/revisione-motore/08-micropallinatura-testa.jpg" alt="Head: before and after micro shot-peening" cap="Before and after micro shot-peening of the head. — In-image text: 'Before and after micro shot-peening.'" %}

## STEP 2 - Measurements: bores, pistons and ring gaps

The heart of the precision work. A **bore gauge** measures the **cylinder bores** (also checking
taper and ovality), a **micrometer** the **piston** diameters: the difference gives the
**piston-to-bore clearance**. Then the **compression ring end gaps** are set. These clearances are
what guarantee sealing, oil control and - above all - no seizures when the engine gets hot and revs.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/revisione-motore/02-misure-canne-pistoni.jpg" alt="Measuring bores and pistons with a bore gauge" cap="Measuring bore and piston diameters, and setting the ring gaps. — In-image text: 'Step 2, RSV4 1000cc engine build: measuring cylinder bore diameters · measuring piston diameters · setting compression-ring gaps.'" %}

## STEP 3 - Balancing the reciprocating masses (pistons)

Each **complete piston assembly** is weighed, then the **individual components**. Material is then
removed from **non-critical spots** to bring all masses to the same value. Balancing the
**reciprocating masses** (the ones going up and down) means fewer vibrations and a smoother engine
that's more usable at high rpm.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/revisione-motore/03-equilibratura-pistoni.jpg" alt="Weighing and balancing the pistons" cap="Pistons, rings and a precision scale, with the weighing sheet. — In-image text: 'Step 3, RSV4 1000cc engine build: weighing the complete pistons · weighing the individual components · then removing material from non-critical spots for a perfect balance of the reciprocating masses.'" %}

## STEP 4 - Balancing the con-rods

Same philosophy for the **connecting rods**: the **total weight** is measured and, separately, the
weight of the **small end** and of the **big end**. That way the **rotating** part (big end) and the
**reciprocating** part (small end) are balanced across all cylinders, again removing material only
from non-critical areas.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/revisione-motore/04-equilibratura-bielle.jpg" alt="Weighing and balancing the con-rods" cap="Measuring small-end and big-end weight on the scale with its dedicated fixture. — In-image text: 'Step 4, RSV4 1000cc engine build: measuring the total con-rod weight · measuring small-end and big-end weight · then balancing by removing material from non-critical areas.'" %}

## STEP 5 - Rings, mating and Plastigauge

Fitting the **rings** with the correct **gap orientation**, **mating pistons to con-rods**, then
closing the bottom end to measure the **main and rod bearing tolerances** with **Plastigauge**.
It's the classic method for checking bearing clearance: the plastic thread gets squashed and its
width gives the real clearance - the oil film (and therefore engine life) depends on it.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/revisione-motore/05-fasce-accoppiamento-plastigauge.jpg" alt="Fitting rings and mating pistons to con-rods" cap="Pistons mated to their rods, ready for the clearance checks. — In-image text: 'Step 5, RSV4 1000cc engine build: fitting the rings and orienting the gaps · mating pistons with con-rods · assembling and closing the bottom end to measure main and rod bearing tolerances with Plastigauge.'" %}

## STEP 6 - Fitting in the block and closing the bottom end

**Pistons and rods go into the block**, the **rod and main bearings are torqued dry** and
re-measured with **Plastigauge** for a **double check** of the tolerances. Then final cleaning,
**assembly lubricant**, **sealing paste (Yamaha ThreeBond)** on the mating surfaces and the
**final closing of the bottom end**.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/revisione-motore/06-monoblocco-threebond.jpg" alt="Fitting in the block, Plastigauge and ThreeBond" cap="Double clearance check and final closing of the crankcases. — In-image text: 'Step 6, RSV4 1000cc engine build: fitting pistons and rods in the block · torquing rod and main bearings dry and measuring with Plastigauge for a double tolerance check · final cleaning, assembly lube · applying Yamaha ThreeBond sealant and final closing of the bottom end.'" %}

## STEP 6.5 - Porting the ducts

In parallel, **porting of the intake and exhaust ducts** on the head (work in progress):
the duct geometry is improved to help the cylinders fill and empty.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/revisione-motore/07-condotti-porting.jpg" alt="Porting the head ducts" cap="Ducts being worked. — In-image text: 'Step 6.5, RSV4 1000cc engine build: porting the intake and exhaust ducts (work in progress).'" %}

## STEP 7 - Heads: milling, valve seats and torquing

The head work is completed: **milling of the intake and exhaust ducts**, **lapping of the valve
seats** (for a perfect seal between valve and seat), **general cleaning**, then **fitting the head
gaskets** and **torquing the heads at 30 + 55 N·m** (two-stage torque).

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/revisione-motore/07b-teste-fresatura-serraggio.jpg" alt="Heads: duct milling, seat lapping and torquing" cap="Duct milling, valve-seat lapping, gaskets and head torquing. — In-image text: 'Step 7, RSV4 1000cc engine build: milling the heads' intake and exhaust ducts · lapping the valve seats · general cleaning · fitting head gaskets and torquing the heads 30+55 Nm.'" %}

## STEP 8 - Timing gear and valve timing

On the closed engine: **fitting the camshafts** and the **timing gear**, **timing both banks**
(front and rear), setting the **valve clearances**, closing the **valve covers** and the
**stator**, and finally fitting the **throttle bodies**.

{% include figure.html src="/assets/img/revisione-motore/09-distribuzione-gioco-valvole.jpg" alt="Reassembled engine: timing gear, bank timing and throttle bodies" cap="The reassembled V4, with timing set and throttle bodies installed. — In-image text: 'Step 8, RSV4 1000cc engine build: fitting camshafts and timing gear · timing the front and rear bank · valve clearances · closing valve covers and stator · fitting the throttle bodies.'" %}

## In conclusion

With the engine closed, the picture is clear: shot-peened crankcases, balanced reciprocating
masses and con-rods, main and rod clearances verified with Plastigauge, worked heads and timing
set. More than a repair, it was a precision job - every dimension measured and every component
weighed before closing it back up.

The V4 is now back in the bike, ready to run.
